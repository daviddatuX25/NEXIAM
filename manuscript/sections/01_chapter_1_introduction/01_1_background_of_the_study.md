# Chapter 1: Introduction

## 1.1 Background of the Study

Education and examination are fundamentally connected. In higher education institutions, admission examinations fulfill two vital functions: they evaluate applicant academic preparedness and regulate institutional access. While psychometric assessment design receives substantial academic attention, the administrative logistics of conducting examination workflows at scale remain comparatively under-researched despite their profound institutional consequences (Woelert, 2023).

Where researchers examine this operational infrastructure directly, they document persistent bottlenecks that compromise the efficiency and equity of the selection process. Patil et al. (2021) observed that large-scale examination administration generates acute difficulties in record integrity, candidate verification, and inter-office coordination, challenges that compound rapidly in paper-based environments. Thakur et al. (2025) extended this assessment through their TrueGradeAI framework, demonstrating that physical, manual testing workflows produce prolonged grading delays and introduce opportunities for subjective error, arguing that end-to-end digital pipelines compress turnaround times from weeks to hours while ensuring operational transparency. Sanchez-Mendiola et al. (2025), examining admission data across more than 600,000 examinees, highlighted the immense administrative resilience an institution must maintain when managing high-stakes testing programs.

This dynamic creates a structural mismatch: institutions invest in standardized assessment instruments but lack the downstream digital architecture to administer sessions, score answer sheets, and disseminate results efficiently. The consequences of this mismatch directly affect institutional enrollment stability. Liu et al. (2025) demonstrated that conventional admission practices often rely on static historical yield assumptions while neglecting statistical uncertainty in applicant decision-making, leaving colleges vulnerable to over- or under-enrollment. Furthermore, the speed of result delivery decisively shapes student enrollment choices: prospective students weighing multiple college offers commit preferentially to whichever institution communicates an official, definitive outcome first (Ruffalo Noel Levitz, 2023). Administrative delays in scoring and notification do not merely inconvenience applicants; they undermine the strategic enrollment management of the institution.

Globally, universities have addressed these operational challenges by deploying cloud-based platforms and artificial intelligence technologies to modernize administrative services (EDUCAUSE, 2023; Marinoni et al., 2020). Automated conversational agents assist in handling routine admissions inquiries at scale, expanding institutional responsiveness without placing continuous burdens on staff (Labadze et al., 2023). Concurrently, Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) has emerged as the standard security paradigm for educational information systems, enforcing strict data isolation so that administrative personnel access only the records their specific organizational functions demand (Sandhu et al., 1996; Kabier et al., 2023).

Within Philippine higher education, digital modernization is driven directly by national policy mandates. The Commission on Higher Education strategic plan establishes digitalization as an institutional priority (CHED, 2022). More critically, Republic Act No. 10931, known as the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act of 2017, dramatically increased applicant volumes across State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) by eliminating tuition and miscellaneous fees. This influx placed unprecedented strain on institutional staff and paper-based infrastructures that did not scale proportionally. At the same time, Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, established stringent legal duties regarding the collection, access, and storage of applicant personal information, requiring educational institutions to implement demonstrable organizational, physical, and technical data protection measures.

At Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College (ISPSC) Tagudin Campus, this administrative pressure is felt acutely. The campus processes between 700 and 1,000 freshman applicants during each annual admission cycle, conducting the entire testing and admission pipeline predominantly through manual, paper-based operations. Empirical inquiries conducted with campus administrators and student applicants reveal severe operational bottlenecks:
1. *Manual Stencil Scoring Delays:* Answer sheets for the ISPSC College Admission Test (ICAT) are scored by hand using cut-out plastic stencils. Guidance personnel confirm that scoring one admission cycle consumes five or more full working days, drawing all available office staff into grading and creating result release backlogs of up to two weeks.
2. *Fragmented Result Dissemination and Mobility Burdens:* The campus maintains no online result retrieval channel. Results are released exclusively during in-person consultation sessions. Applicants rely on an office-hours-only Facebook page for batch announcements. Empirical survey data from current examinees indicates that sixty percent of applicants travel from municipalities outside Tagudin (such as Sta. Cruz and Alilem), making at least three separate physical campus trips during the cycle and absorbing substantial travel expenses.
3. *Informal Coordination Channels:* Examination batch scheduling and classroom allocations between the Guidance Office and the Registrar Office are coordinated through informal text messages and social media chats. Seventy percent of surveyed applicants learned their examination schedules solely from social media posts, with thirty percent experiencing short notice or schedule conflicts.
4. *Undocumented Counseling Sessions and Queue Strains:* Post-examination consultation sessions process 70 to 80 applicants per day, with interviews lasting only 2 to 5 minutes. Counselors provide career advice orally without creating persistent records. Sixty percent of applicants wait in open queues for thirty minutes to half a day, and twenty percent report finding their preferred academic programs completely full upon enrollment due to a total lack of real-time capacity transparency.
5. *Absence of Persistent Configuration Records:* Cycle parameters, room capacities, department quotas, and intake documents exist only on loose paper lists and in staff memory, with seventy percent of applicants experiencing uncertainty regarding document verification.

Prior research conducted at ISPSC Tagudin Campus has explored isolated aspects of institutional computing:
* Eustaquio and Nisperos constructed a web-based student registration system with basic decision-support features.
* Angala et al. (2023), Cenezan and Gacusan (2025), and Cenezan and Valdez (2023) developed digital solutions for registrar document tracking and academic clearance workflows.
* Yukee et al. (2025) successfully clustered student ICAT scores using the K-Means algorithm, and Ballesteros et al. (2025) grouped student profiles using academic and socioeconomic indicators.

While these local studies demonstrated the viability of educational data analysis at ISPSC Tagudin Campus, they operated as retrospective research exercises on archived, historical data rather than operational systems integrated into live admission cycles. In particular, the applicant profiling approaches developed by Yukee et al. and Ballesteros et al. expose a critical methodological dilemma: classical machine learning models require thousands of training records and fixed data tables. In campus environments where annual cohorts rarely exceed 700 applicants, training supervised algorithms or relying solely on uncalibrated clustering introduces severe risks of overfitting and historical bias (Van Busum & Fang, 2025; Mittal & Malhotra, 2026). Furthermore, incoming freshmen often present incomplete secondary records, such as missing specialized mathematics or science marks, which traditional algorithms cannot evaluate without synthetic penalties or imputation distortion.

Recent advances in tabular foundation models resolve this operational tension. Pre-trained Tabular Prior-data Fitted Networks (TabPFN) perform Bayesian in-context learning directly on tabular data in a single forward pass without requiring local parameter retraining (Hollmann et al., 2025). By evaluating candidate profiles against verified institutional curriculum benchmarks while natively accommodating missing secondary features through explicit missingness channels, foundation model triage provides immediate, calibrated decision support for guidance personnel during live counseling.

To date, no single platform has unified applicant intake, examination scheduling, computer-vision OMR scoring, in-context decision support, controlled result release, and cross-office coordination into a unified, role-governed pipeline. This study addresses that institutional gap through the development and evaluation of NEXIAM.
