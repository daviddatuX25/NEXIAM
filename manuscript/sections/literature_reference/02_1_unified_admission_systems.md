# Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature and Studies

## 2.1 Unified Admission Systems and Institutional Workflow Modernization

The modernization of higher education admissions has increasingly centered on unifying previously disconnected administrative workflows into integrated digital platforms. As universities transition away from fragmented physical record keeping, researchers have documented both the architectural benefits and the human factors that govern unified system adoption.

In a comprehensive international study, Adwan et al. (2023) developed and evaluated a unified mobile-based admission system serving multiple colleges and academic departments across the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region. Utilizing an Agile system development lifecycle closely paralleling modern Rapid Application Development, their empirical evaluation revealed a 97.7% usability score measured against Nielsen heuristics. The authors demonstrated that replacing isolated departmental application points with a centralized digital gateway eliminates redundant applicant encoding, prevents data transcription errors between administrative units, and accelerates institutional response times. Their findings validated the proposition that administrative efficiency in higher education depends fundamentally on cross-departmental integration rather than on piecemeal software tools.

In a complementary investigation of institutional web platforms, Rutvik et al. (2024) examined the implementation of a web-based college admission system designed to digitize application intake, document verification, and result dissemination. Their findings highlighted that web-based portals reduce physical campus crowding during peak admission periods, providing applicants with transparent tracking of their application status while relieving administrative staff of repetitive walk-in inquiries. However, the authors noted that basic web portals often fail when testing and psychometric scoring workflows are treated as external, disconnected activities, emphasizing the necessity of integrating testing operations directly into the admissions software architecture.

The operational challenges of running examination logistics in state universities were investigated by Nafrees and Kariapper (2020), who evaluated an online management information system deployed within the examination departments of state universities in Sri Lanka. Their study documented that state institutions in developing countries face unique infrastructure constraints, including intermittent network connectivity, limited departmental hardware, and heavy applicant surges during open enrollment windows. The researchers concluded that institutional systems must be engineered with lightweight web architectures, robust offline resilience for on-campus testing sessions, and clear role differentiation to prevent administrative deadlocks.

In the context of the Philippine higher education sector, the demand for unified admissions platforms is driven by intense structural pressures. As documented by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED, 2022), state universities and colleges (SUCs) have experienced unprecedented application volumes following the enactment of the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (Republic Act No. 10931). When tuition fees were removed, applicant numbers expanded exponentially, while administrative staffing levels remained relatively static. Consequently, institutions operating on physical paperwork have seen their scoring and counseling pipelines severely strained.

While baseline registration systems have been explored in local Philippine contexts, such as the initial student registration prototypes developed by Eustaquio and Nisperos, regional state college campuses have historically operated testing and registration as separate, non-communicating silos. Guidance personnel administer and score entrance tests independently, transferring results to the Registrar Office through verbal communications or physical clearance slips. The unified admission platform literature demonstrates that substantial operational gains occur only when applicant registration, examination scheduling, psychometric scoring, and enrollment verification operate as integrated stages of a single, auditable workflow. NEXIAM builds directly upon this principle, establishing a role-isolated pipeline specifically tailored to the operational realities of a Philippine state college campus.
