# Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature and Studies

## 2.5 Educational Conversational Agents and Retrieval-Augmented Generation

The proliferation of digital communication channels in higher education has created substantial administrative demands on admissions personnel. During peak application and examination windows, guidance and registrar staff are frequently overwhelmed by high-volume, repetitive inquiries regarding admission deadlines, required credentials, examination schedules, and scoring clearances. To alleviate staff exhaustion while ensuring timely applicant communication, educational institutions have increasingly deployed conversational artificial intelligence agents.

In a comprehensive survey of educational support systems, Labadze et al. (2023) analyzed the role of artificial intelligence chatbots in university administration. The authors observed that traditional static FAQ web pages often fail to satisfy prospective students, who increasingly expect interactive, conversational assistance available around the clock. By handling routine procedural inquiries automatically, conversational agents expand institutional coverage across weekends and evening hours, allowing professional staff to dedicate their time to complex counseling and case-specific evaluations. Similar findings were documented by Neupane et al., who showed that domain-informed campus chatbots significantly reduced walk-in traffic and telephone inquiries in university admissions offices.

However, standard generative language models present a critical vulnerability in institutional settings: the tendency to hallucinate unverified or inaccurate information when responding to specialized policy questions. In university admissions, where procedural deadlines and document requirements carry high stakes for applicants, a conversational model generating inaccurate instructions can compromise student eligibility and expose the institution to regulatory disputes.

To resolve this limitation, computer scientists developed the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture (Lewis et al., 2020). Rather than relying on a general-purpose language model to generate answers solely from its internal parametric memory, RAG couples the generation model with a structured information retrieval pipeline. When an applicant submits a query, the system first retrieves the most relevant semantic text passages from an authoritative institutional knowledge base. The language model is then instructed to synthesize its response strictly from the retrieved source passages, citing specific policy provisions and eliminating the generation of fabricated details.

The efficacy of RAG in Philippine higher education was empirically demonstrated by Dayata et al., who developed and evaluated the Carolinian Chatbot for students at the University of San Carlos. Their research confirmed that grounding the generative conversational pipeline in verified university handbooks, academic policies, and curriculum guides dramatically improved answer accuracy while earning high usability and usefulness ratings from student evaluators. The authors emphasized that successful educational chatbots must incorporate explicit refusal boundaries: when a student inquiry falls outside the scope of the institutional knowledge base, the chatbot must gracefully decline to answer and direct the student to appropriate human staff rather than attempting speculative generation.

These architectural principles directly govern the informational chatbot integrated into NEXIAM. At ISPSC Tagudin Campus, Guidance personnel reported that public communication relies heavily on an office-hours-only Facebook page, leaving student inquiries unanswered during evenings and weekends and pushing applicants to make repeated campus trips merely to clarify basic procedures. NEXIAM deploys a policy-grounded RAG chatbot to resolve this communication bottleneck:
1. *Corpus Grounding:* The retrieval index is populated strictly with approved institutional documents, including the official ISPSC admission guidelines, campus examination venue directories, and academic program profiles.
2. *Confidence-Gated Refusal:* When the semantic relevance of retrieved passages falls below a configurable confidence threshold, the chatbot explicitly states that it lacks verified information, preventing misleading guidance.
3. *Case-Attached Human Escalation:* For procedural inquiries that the chatbot cannot resolve, the platform provides an in-app inquiry submission mechanism that binds the student's question directly to their official applicant profile, routing the concern to staff for attributable human resolution.
