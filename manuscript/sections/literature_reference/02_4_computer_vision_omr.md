# Chapter 2: Review of Related Literature and Studies

## 2.4 Computer-Vision Optical Mark Recognition and Assessment Infrastructure

The evaluation of standardized examinations in educational institutions has undergone a technological transition from manual human checking to dedicated optical mark readers, and ultimately to software-defined computer-vision Optical Mark Recognition (OMR) executed on commodity hardware. Examining this literature clarifies the engineering justification for the scoring engine implemented in NEXIAM.

The operational vulnerabilities of physical, paper-based examination management were detailed by Patil et al. (2021), who investigated record integrity, applicant verification, and administrative coordination in large-scale academic testing programs. Their findings established that physical examination workflows generate acute logistical pressures during the grading phase. When examination sheets are evaluated manually or transferred physically across administrative offices, institutions experience substantial turnaround delays, transcription errors during manual score encoding, and an absence of verifiable audit logs to resolve student grade disputes. The authors concluded that securing assessment integrity requires digitizing both the grading mechanism and the score custody pipeline.

In a comprehensive study of automated grading architectures, Thakur et al. (2025) proposed the TrueGradeAI framework, analyzing how paper-based assessment workflows compromise institutional efficiency. The researchers documented that manual checking introduces subjective fatigue and inconsistent scoring thresholds, with physical grading cycles often consuming multiple weeks in large applicant cohorts. By contrast, deploying automated computer-vision scoring compressed grading turnarounds from weeks to hours while providing mathematical transparency and eliminating transcription discrepancies between physical answer sheets and institutional databases.

The viability of software-based OMR in developing educational settings was validated by Cuerdo (2023), who investigated the adoption of the EvalBee computer-vision OMR application across educational institutions in the Philippines. Traditional optical mark readers required specialized, expensive scanning hardware and pre-printed proprietary answer sheets with strict optical ink specifications, placing automated scoring out of reach for resource-constrained provincial schools. Cuerdo demonstrated that modern computer-vision algorithms operating on standard flatbed document scanners or smartphone cameras could reliably detect marked bubbles on standard paper sheets printed on ordinary office equipment. The study confirmed that software-based OMR achieved high grading accuracy and widespread teacher acceptance, proving that commodity hardware can successfully replace dedicated scanning appliances in Philippine schools.

Further technical validations in automated testing were contributed by Catalan et al., who demonstrated that robust image preprocessing (including grayscale conversion, adaptive thresholding, morphological contour detection, and perspective transformation) enables software engines to align scanned answer sheets accurately even when physical pages are slightly skewed during batch scanning.

These findings directly inform the assessment architecture of NEXIAM. At ISPSC Tagudin Campus, entrance examination grading has historically relied on plastic stencils, occupying Guidance personnel for more than five days per cycle and leaving the office unable to log or mathematically quantify scoring errors. NEXIAM resolves this operational bottleneck by integrating an open-source computer-vision scoring pipeline (OMRChecker) operating on commodity office scanners:
1. *Perspective and Homography Correction:* The engine detects four corner alignment markers on the answer sheet, mathematically correcting rotational tilt and perspective distortion before evaluating bubble coordinates.
2. *Confidence-Gated Bubble Detection:* The system calculates pixel fill densities for each multiple-choice bubble. Marked answers falling within clear fill thresholds are scored automatically, while ambiguous markings (such as faint pencil erasures or dual-marked choices) are flagged for human adjudication.
3. *Attributable Human Verification Workbench:* Scored sheets flagged with low-confidence bubbles are routed to an administrative review screen where guidance personnel inspect high-resolution crops of the disputed bubbles, ensuring human oversight over ambiguous examinee responses.
4. *Complete Manual Fallback:* In strict alignment with institutional resilience requirements, the platform maintains a full manual score entry interface, guaranteeing that scoring operations continue uninterrupted even in the event of scanner hardware failures.
