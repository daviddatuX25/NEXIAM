# Final Security, Privacy, and Data-Integrity Review

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-10  
**Primary artifact:** `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`  
**Supporting artifacts:** `JUNIOR-IMPLEMENTATION-GUIDE.md`, `READINESS-PLAN.md`  
**Verdict:** **CONDITIONAL PASS** — the spine is suitable as the architectural authority for planning, but the implementation OpenSpec must close the data-release, ingestion, custody, and recovery findings below before live provider data, RAG, or mutation capabilities are promoted.

## Findings

- **Streaming currently permits an unguarded release path.** AD-11 describes validation, citation/support checks, sanitization, and typed projection before provider output reaches the user, while AD-17 and the frontend guide allow transient `text_delta` events. The contract does not state whether raw provider deltas are buffered or incrementally inspected before display. A provider can emit PII, injected instructions, unsafe links, unsupported claims, or system-prompt fragments before an end-of-answer validator rejects the result. The implementation contract must require either server-side buffering until the complete answer passes the Prompt/Data Firewall or a specified incremental release gate that applies classification, secret/PII detection, escaping, and claim/citation rules before every visible delta; a later terminal refusal cannot retract already disclosed bytes.

- **Knowledge upload and extraction lack a hostile-file boundary.** AD-25 governs source lifecycle, checksums, review, and publication, and the guide mentions upload scanning as a UI state, but neither artifact defines quarantine, content-based MIME detection, archive/decompression limits, file/page/character limits, encrypted temporary storage, malware scanning, parser/OCR isolation, timeout/memory limits, macro/active-content rejection, filename normalization, or cleanup after failed extraction. URL ingestion is not explicitly prohibited or SSRF-contained. The knowledge OpenSpec must make ingestion a quarantined, resource-bounded, non-networked worker path and prove malicious archive, polyglot, parser-crash, and cleanup cases before CRUD is exposed.

- **Approval does not explicitly bind every publication-relevant byte and field.** A checksum and immutable extraction artifact are required, but the architecture does not clearly say that approval binds the source bytes, extracted text, redactions, chunks/anchors, audience, purpose, classification, institution, effective dates, supersession target, citation fixtures, and index profile as one canonical digest. It also does not state that changing any bound item invalidates prior approval and returns the version to review. Without that rule, a legitimate approval can be reused after a metadata or extraction change. Add an immutable `KnowledgePublicationCandidate` digest and require fresh independent approval after any bound-field change.

- **Knowledge separation of duties is policy-optional where the dangerous transitions need an exact baseline.** AD-16 and AD-25 say separation of duties applies “where required,” while the guide assumes an independent approver. The minimum invariant is not defined for self-approval, delegated/temporary authority, emergency publication, approver revocation after approval, or the same actor controlling owner, approver, index operator, and purge roles. The OpenSpec must publish a transition-by-transition actor matrix, default-deny self-approval for active corpus publication, bind approver authority at decision time, and specify any break-glass exception with dual control, expiry, alerting, and retrospective review.

- **Protected-read grants cap individual calls but not aggregate disclosure.** AD-6 binds maximum cardinality/pages to a short-lived invocation grant, yet a model can issue many individually valid reads across steps, retries, turns, overlapping filters, or concurrent runs and reconstruct a larger staff/applicant dataset through enumeration or differencing. Step/token budgets are cost controls, not privacy controls. Introduce a server-owned disclosure budget bound to actor, institution, purpose, context epoch/TaskGrant, projection, unique targets, cumulative rows/fields, and time window; count cached/retried/overlapping reads deterministically and test multi-call enumeration and aggregate inference.

- **Pseudonymization and durable AI evidence have no explicit custody controls.** AD-10/AD-11 require pseudonymous references and a retained pseudonym map, while proposals, conversations, citations, protected-view evidence, knowledge snapshots, and tool-call metadata can remain sensitive even without raw prompts. The spine does not require encryption at rest, key ownership/rotation, column-level access policy, backup encryption, privileged-read audit, or separation between operators and plaintext mappings. Define a data-class-to-store matrix covering encryption, keys, read roles, retention, legal hold, export, erasure, backup expiry, and provider-side retention for each durable record class before live data is allowed.

- **Replay and snapshot authorization needs to be explicitly equivalent to a fresh read.** AD-17 says access remains authorized and AD-21 invalidates revoked citations, but the protocol does not require every replay/snapshot payload to be reprojected under current surface, actor, scope, release state, purpose, source eligibility, and retention policy. Stored durable events may contain text or citation spans that were lawful when created but are no longer lawful after duty expiry, applicant release reversal, role change, source revocation, or policy narrowing. Require replay to emit current safe projections or tombstones—not stored payload bytes—and add tests for all of those changes during reconnect and history access.

- **Conversation clearing, retention erasure, audit preservation, and backups lack one deletion graph.** “Clear history,” retention erasure reconciliation, knowledge deletion, immutable audit, usage ledgers, provider retention, caches, indexes, exports, and backups are described separately. The architecture does not define which records are erased, pseudonymized, tombstoned, retained under legal/audit obligation, or reported as pending, nor how erasure propagates to provider-held data. Add a record-level retention/deletion matrix and an idempotent deletion graph with hold precedence, evidence of downstream completion, user-visible semantics for “clear,” and restore-time prevention of resurrecting deleted content.

- **The audit contract is not yet tamper-evident or independently verifiable.** Transactions own audit/outbox writes and `PrivacyDecision` is hash-linked, but the spine does not require append-only enforcement, hash chaining or signed checkpoints, restricted audit administration, clock/sequence integrity, independent export, or verification after restore. A database administrator or compromised application credential could alter the evidence used to investigate provider egress or confirmed commands. Define tamper-evidence and verification requirements for consent, protected reads, privacy decisions, proposals, confirmations, admin overrides, knowledge transitions, usage settlement, break-glass, and reconciliation records.

- **Partial bulk execution needs a crash-safe item protocol.** AD-8 requires declared atomic/partial semantics and per-item outcomes, but it does not define stable per-item idempotency identities, checkpoint ordering, stop/cancel behavior after some commits, retry selection, proposal-impact reconciliation, or how a restored worker distinguishes committed items from uncommitted items. A single batch idempotency key is insufficient after a crash. Require an immutable item manifest, per-item version/idempotency binding, transactional checkpoint/outbox ordering, deterministic resume, explicit cancellation semantics, and operator-visible reconciliation whose totals equal the proposal impact set.

- **Disaster recovery does not yet prove exactly-once observable outcomes.** AD-18 names reconciliation jobs, but there is no RPO/RTO, restore ordering, backup scope, or rehearsal contract for proposals in `executing`, usage reservations, stream cursors, audit/outbox, knowledge active pointers, revocation predicates, pseudonym maps, and idempotency records. Restoring mismatched snapshots can duplicate a confirmed command, reopen a revoked source, replay stale citations, or lose usage/evidence. The operations OpenSpec must define a consistency group and restore sequence, fail closed while reconciliation is incomplete, and rehearse backup/restore with pending confirmations, partial batches, active revocation, and provider timeout-after-output.

- **Administrative policy changes need transactional effective-time semantics.** The deny-dominant resolver is sound, but quota/availability/routing publications do not specify how effective timestamps, timezone changes, outstanding reservations, in-flight runs, cached policy, or concurrent override edits are serialized. Lowering a ceiling below reserved usage or changing provider profile mid-run can produce negative availability, mixed destination policy, or misleading dashboards. Bind each run/reservation to an immutable policy/pricing/routing version, define prospective versus immediate fields, use optimistic versioning for admin commands, and specify settlement/reconciliation under superseded policies.

- **Surface isolation requires storage-level constraints in addition to resolver tests.** AD-23 correctly isolates staff, applicant, proctor, knowledge-admin, operations, and break-glass profiles, but the persistence model does not state composite ownership keys or database constraints that prevent an ID collision or faulty query from associating a conversation, context epoch, consent, TaskGrant, proposal, or stream cursor with another actor type/guard/institution/profile. The foundation OpenSpec must define non-null composite tenancy/actor/surface keys, foreign-key or equivalent integrity constraints, globally opaque external IDs, and repository methods that require the full ownership tuple; test both current dual-auth guards and concurrent profile sessions.

- **Mode consent needs an explicit withdrawal and stale-tab contract.** Consent is well bound at creation, and the UI offers demotion, but there is no exact rule for revoking consent across concurrent tabs/devices, whether pending read/tool work is cancelled, and how a stale Assistant tab learns that the user withdrew or narrowed consent elsewhere. Define one monotonic consent/version record per session/surface/task grant, recheck it before provider egress as well as tool/confirm boundaries, emit revocation/tombstone events, and ensure stale tabs cannot continue streaming protected output.

- **Prompt-injection tests do not yet cover persistence and indirect re-entry paths.** The suite names user, corpus, and tool-result injection, but generated summaries, uploaded document metadata, citation labels, safe error text, prior assistant messages, admin-authored descriptions, and persisted proposal impact text can later re-enter prompts or UI. Require provenance labels on every natural-language segment, prohibit model output from becoming a system/developer instruction or capability description, reapply the Prompt/Data Firewall on stored-content reuse, and include multi-turn delayed injection, encoded/obfuscated instructions, and compromised-document metadata in the adversarial corpus.

## Positive controls preserved

- One shared Capability Kernel avoids duplicate AI authorization and business routes.
- Provider-facing tools exclude commands; persisted proposals, fresh authorization, canonical rule evidence, compare-and-swap, and ordinary Actions remain the mutation boundary.
- Surface profiles, context epochs, TaskGrants, and deterministic own/staff data Queries establish the correct isolation model.
- RAG is limited to governed policy sources with auth-before-retrieve; operational/applicant records are not embedded for convenience.
- Synchronous source-revocation denial, deny-dominant policy, manual fallbacks, bounded orchestration, and reconciliation provide a strong safe-failure baseline.

## Promotion condition

The findings above do not require redesigning the central architecture. They require explicit contracts and acceptance tests in the relevant foundation, knowledge, provider, RAG, applicant, mutation, and operations OpenSpecs. Live provider data must remain gated until the streaming-release, data-custody, replay, retention, and recovery contracts are accepted; knowledge CRUD must remain gated until hostile-file ingestion and approval-binding rules are accepted; mutation rollout must remain gated until bulk crash recovery and audit verification are accepted.
