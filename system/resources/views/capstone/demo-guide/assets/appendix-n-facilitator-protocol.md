## 1. Protocol Overview and Purpose

This protocol standardizes preparation, briefing, task observation, and post-test administration for the NEXIAM simulation scenarios. The planned participants are three Registrar personnel, two Guidance personnel, three academic leaders (one Program Head or Dean from each of CAS, CBME, and CTE), and 255 first-year students, for a planned total of 263. The student population and selection basis remain unverified and must be resolved before recruitment or claims about representativeness.

Participants complete assigned tasks and then answer one post-test questionnaire about usefulness (PU) and ease of use (PEOU). The session is not a performance examination of participants. Task completion, system errors, assistance, and unavailable or unauthorized tasks are recorded separately from questionnaire responses. Do not coach participants or suggest questionnaire answers.

The four scenarios are: A, first-year student; B, Registrar; C, Guidance; and D, academic leader. Each item in Appendix J maps to an observation task below. Use only items within the participant's assigned tasks and permissions. An untested, unavailable, or unauthorized function is not rated Neutral. Participants may rate a function they attempted even when they could not complete its task; retain the failure as a separate observation.

## 2. Preparation and Safety Checks

### 2.1 Preparation is a researcher responsibility

Researchers prepare the answer-sheet design and mappings, answer keys, score-conversion tables, and benchmarks required for the scenarios. Staff evaluators do not prepare or validate this configuration as research tasks. Before scheduling a session, researchers confirm that the required configurations are approved and suitable for the isolated simulation. Record missing approvals, incomplete pilots, and other unresolved prerequisites without implying that they have passed.

Ordinary scoring of prepared answer sheets is a technical prerequisite for Guidance's scoring task and the generated result sheet used in the face-to-face consultation scenario. It is separate from TAM measurement. Do not ask participants to adjudicate uncertain marks, validate setup, or assess scoring accuracy. Do not make a camera, device, recognition-accuracy, or model-performance claim based on this protocol.

### 2.2 Pre-session gates

Before inviting participants to a session, the lead researcher and the relevant system owner must check and record:

1. The approved study protocol, questionnaire, item wording, and pilot status. If approval or pilot work is incomplete, do not begin data collection.
2. The permitted environment and policy mode for the planned tasks. Confirm that the environment is isolated from live institutional records and that the applicable privacy, applicant-communication, result-release, and consultation rules allow each task.
3. Researcher-prepared, synthetic fixtures for all needed roles and scenarios. Verify their actual state, labels, relationships, and expected starting pages through the owning feature handoff or an authorized owner. Do not assume a seeder ran, that a fixture exists, or that an ID, account, credential, result, referral, schedule, or thread is ready.
4. Synthetic participant accounts and role permissions, using the approved access procedure. Verify each account's actual authentication path and task-specific permissions before the session. Do not invent or reuse credentials from a handoff unless that handoff explicitly verifies them for this fixture and purpose.
5. The required ordinary-scoring output, generated physical result sheet, face-to-face consultation context, applicant-specific Guidance-to-academic-leader thread, and authorized Registrar handoff information, as applicable. Confirm that every item is allowed for the assigned role and policy mode. The Registrar reviews only handoff information authorized for that role; this does not grant access to a restricted conversation thread.
6. That each task can be performed without real applicant data, sending a real notice, changing live records, or affecting another participant's work. Prepare an isolated synthetic fixture path approved by its owner. Never run an unconditional destructive reset or overwrite a shared environment to create a session baseline.
7. A neutral task card, a separate observation record, a private questionnaire, and a procedure for recording technical failures and assistance without exposing one participant's responses to another.

This document does not verify an end-to-end evaluation fixture or its role permissions. Before the session, check the actual synthetic records, scoring and result-sheet path, and paired communication task. Do not infer readiness from a completed implementation checklist. If a required function is unavailable, record the limitation and resolve the session plan before recruitment rather than substitute a different function.

### 2.3 Session setup

Use the approved, isolated workstation and policy mode. Open only the verified starting page for the assigned scenario. Keep account and fixture instructions private to the participant who needs them. Confirm that no live data, unrelated staff account, real applicant conversation, or production record is visible. Do not state that the system, inference service, fixture, or data is ready unless that was checked for the specific session and documented.

## 3. Standardized Verbal Briefing

Read this statement verbatim before participants begin. Do not add persuasive commentary or coach participants through tasks.

> "Good morning or afternoon. Thank you for considering participation in this research evaluation.
>
> You are invited to try selected NEXIAM tasks for an undergraduate capstone study. This session evaluates the software, not your personal ability. There are no right or wrong questionnaire answers.
>
> Your participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time. Your questionnaire responses will be handled according to the approved study and privacy procedures and reported in aggregate form where appropriate.
>
> Please follow the task card and work independently. I will observe whether each task is completed, attempted, unavailable, or interrupted, and will record any technical problem or assistance separately. I cannot explain how to complete a task or recommend a questionnaire rating. If there is a technical interruption, please let me know. After the tasks, complete the questionnaire independently. An item you could not test should be left unanswered, not rated Neutral.
>
> Please use only the synthetic information provided for this session. Do not enter real applicant information or send messages outside the approved simulation. Thank you."

## 4. Standardized Scenario Task Cards

Only present a scenario after its pre-session gates pass. The researcher may clarify the wording of a task card but must not demonstrate the interface, lead the participant to a control, or suggest a response. Participants work independently. Record task status and any assistance in the observation table in Section 5.

## 5. Observation and Task-Status Procedure

An observer records behavior without directing it. Use one row per task, role, and participant code; do not write participant names or real applicant data in the observation sheet. Record the observed outcome, not an interpretation of the participant's ability.

Use these task statuses:

- **Completed:** The participant reached the task's stated outcome without facilitator guidance.
- **Attempted, not completed:** The participant made an observable attempt but did not reach the stated outcome.
- **Blocked: technical:** A technical failure prevented or interrupted the task. Describe the failure briefly and record any technical assistance.
- **Unavailable: fixture:** The expected synthetic data or state was not present or verifiable.
- **Not permitted:** The role, policy mode, approval, or owner authorization did not permit the task.
- **Not attempted:** The task was not presented or started; record why.

For each row, record the start and end status only if these are directly observed and useful; do not claim elapsed task times from estimates. Record assistance separately: what the participant requested, what the facilitator did, and whether the participant resumed. Assistance must not include navigation instructions, explanation of system behavior, or questionnaire coaching. Do not discard task failures or assistance records when summarizing observations.

## 6. Facilitator Rules and Non-Interference

1. **No coaching:** Do not demonstrate a task, explain why a feature exists, point to a control, or recommend a rating. If asked for help completing a task, say: *"Please continue as you understand the task card. I can record what happened, but I cannot guide you through the system."*
2. **Technical assistance only:** For a browser crash, network interruption, or hardware issue, record the incident and the assistance provided. Do not change participant input or silently restart a failed task. Resume only if the participant chooses and the approved procedure permits it.
3. **Independent voluntary responses:** Participants complete the questionnaire individually, without group discussion. They may decline any question or stop participation. Do not pressure them to complete a task or response.
4. **Keep evidence separate:** Keep task observations, technical-support records, and questionnaire responses separate and coded according to the approved research procedure. Do not inspect responses in a way that influences the participant or other participants.
5. **No destructive reset:** Do not run `migrate:fresh`, broad reset, reseeding, or any other destructive command as a session-preparation shortcut. Between sessions, use only the owner-approved isolated fixture and cleanup procedure, after verifying the target environment. If no such procedure is available, stop and consult the owner rather than modifying data.
6. **Preserve failures:** Record missing fixtures, denied access, failed actions, software errors, and assistance. Do not quietly exclude these observations or convert nonperformance into a neutral questionnaire answer.
